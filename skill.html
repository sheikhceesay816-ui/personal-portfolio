My Journey into Formal Education

Before entering formal education, I had spent many years studying in the traditional Dara system. Dara education was an informal form of learning, but it gave me a strong foundation in Qur’anic and Islamic studies. During this period, I studied many important books and subjects, including the Qur’an, Hadith, Nahw (Arabic grammar), and other areas of Islamic knowledge.

Later, someone advised me that I should also pursue formal education and obtain at least a recognized certificate, as this could be useful for my future. I took this advice seriously and decided to begin a new stage of my education.

I enrolled at a school called MAHAND Seydil Haji Malik Sy in Tivaouane. I chose to register as an independent candidate, which meant that I was responsible for preparing myself for the examination. I registered on my own, obtained the necessary books, and began studying independently.

I worked hard to prepare for the examination. I purchased the required books and studied them by myself, relying on my own discipline and determination. When the examination period arrived, I went to the examination centre and completed the exams.

When the results were eventually released, I was very happy to discover that I had passed. This success became an important turning point in my educational journey. It gave me confidence and encouraged me to continue pursuing formal education alongside the Islamic education I had already received.

From that point onward, I continued developing myself through both formal education and the knowledge I had gained through the traditional Dara system.

My Return to The Gambia and the Beginning of My Teaching Journey

During the period of the COVID-19 pandemic, life became difficult in many places, including where I was studying. The challenges of that period affected our daily lives and education.

In 2020, an opportunity arose that changed the direction of my journey. My Sheikh, Ibrahim Njie, who was the Imam of the Zawiya of Sheikh Sidi Al-Haji Malik, informed me about a new school in The Gambia that needed someone to teach the Qur’an and Arabic. He asked me whether I would be interested in going there to teach.

I accepted the opportunity because I saw it as a chance to return to The Gambia and use the knowledge I had gained through my years of study to teach others. I therefore returned to The Gambia and joined Darul Arkam, a school located in Foni, where I began teaching the Qur’an and Arabic.

At that time, I had also been considering travelling to Mauritania to continue my studies. However, the opportunity to teach in The Gambia changed my plans and brought me back home.

After returning to The Gambia, I also became concerned about obtaining a formal Gambian educational certificate. Although I had acquired extensive knowledge through the Dara system and my studies in Senegal, I wanted to obtain a recognized certificate that could support my future educational and professional development.

I first approached Tadamun School and discussed my situation with its director. However, they explained that the arrangement I was seeking was not possible. I continued looking for another opportunity that would allow me to pursue formal education while maintaining my existing studies and responsibilities.

Eventually, I approached Umm al-Qura, where I was given an opportunity to follow their educational programme while continuing my Dara studies. I could study independently and then attend the school when examinations were scheduled. Through this arrangement, I was able to complete the necessary examinations and eventually obtain my higher education certificate.

This experience taught me an important lesson: education can take different forms, and traditional Islamic learning and formal education can complement each other. My journey allowed me to continue studying the Islamic sciences while also working toward formal qualifications that could help me in the future.
