My Early Education

My education began under the guidance of my parents, particularly my father, who was my first Qur’anic teacher. I did not begin this journey alone. I studied together with two other learners: Abdurahman, who is my aunt’s son, and another student named Karebi. The three of us learned together under my father’s guidance.

At that time, there was no electricity in our village, so studying at night was not easy. I still remember how we would go into the forest to collect firewood. We would bring the firewood home and burn it at night so that the light from the fire could help us see while we were studying. Although those conditions were difficult, I remember that period of my life with great happiness. It was a simple time, but it was also a very meaningful part of my education.

During those early years, our main focus was memorization. I was able to memorize my lessons quickly, and I believe that Allah blessed me with a good memory and a sharp mind. I could sometimes learn a lesson within a short period and memorize it. This ability encouraged me to continue learning and strengthened my interest in Qur’anic education.

In addition to my Qur’anic studies, I also had some early exposure to French. There was a school in the village where I had the opportunity to learn some French. One of the teachers I remember from that period was Mr. Moussa Baji. Although my early education was mainly focused on the Qur’an, this experience also introduced me to another area of learning.

These experiences formed an important part of my childhood education. Before my father eventually sent me to Senegal to continue my studies, I had already developed a strong foundation in Qur’anic learning, memorization, and a desire to continue seeking knowledge.

My Journey to Senegal and Qur’anic Studies

Later in my childhood, my parents moved from our village to a town. At that time, there were limited opportunities for farming, and my father began thinking about the future of our education. He decided that the best path for us was to continue our Qur’anic and Islamic education in Senegal.

My journey to Senegal was not undertaken alone. I travelled with my younger brothers, including Aladibara, who is now a businessman, and Aladikabu, as well as my cousin Abdurahman. Our destination was the Thiès region of Senegal, specifically the Tivaouane area, which was well known for Islamic learning and scholarship. We went there with the purpose of continuing our study of the Qur’an and gaining a deeper understanding of Islamic knowledge.

When we first arrived, we did not know anyone there. Our parents also did not have close connections in the area, apart from knowing the Sheikh to whom they entrusted us. My father did not know the Sheikh very well, but he trusted him and believed that sending us there would give us an opportunity to pursue our education. With that trust and hope, he left us in Senegal so that we could continue learning.

Life there was not always easy. We had to adapt to a completely new environment and learn how to live away from our parents. We faced various difficulties while continuing our studies. I remember one occasion when my father came to visit us and found that we had developed wounds on our hands. He was deeply concerned and sympathetic when he saw our condition. Nevertheless, he continued to trust in Allah and remained hopeful that the difficulties we were experiencing would eventually lead to something beneficial.

Despite the challenges, we continued our education. Eventually, I left Tivaouane and travelled to another village called Koki, a place that is well known in Senegal for Qur’anic and Islamic learning. My time in Koki became a very important stage in my educational journey. It was there that I continued my Qur’anic studies and eventually completed the memorization of the Holy Qur’an.

Completing the Qur’an was a major milestone in my life. It was the result of years of learning, memorization, patience, discipline, and the support and sacrifices of my parents. Looking back, I understand that the difficulties I experienced during those years were part of a much greater journey of seeking knowledge.

My Continued Qur’anic and Islamic Education

After completing the memorization of the Holy Qur’an in Koki, I faced an important moment in my educational journey. At that time, my main teacher in Koki was Abidinu Binan. He informed me that I had completed my Qur’anic memorization and that it was time for me to move on to other areas of learning.

However, I was not ready to accept that I had finished. I felt that I still needed more time with the Qur’an, so I asked to remain and repeat my memorization for a few more months. My teacher explained to me that I had already completed it and encouraged me to begin studying other subjects. At that time, I disagreed with him and decided to leave Koki and move to another village.

My parents did not know where I had gone. When they realized that I was missing, they became concerned and began searching for me. They contacted people in different places, trying to find out where I was. At one point, my parents even sent my brother to Senegal to search for me in different villages.

Eventually, I returned to Tivaouane. There, I completed what is known as Tasmi', in which I recited the entire Qur’an from memory without looking at the text. This allowed my teachers to confirm that I had indeed memorized the Qur’an. I completed this recitation before the occasion of Eid al-Adha, commonly known as Tabaski.

After Tabaski, I returned to Koki, where I continued my studies for some time before eventually returning to Tivaouane. I had developed a strong connection with Tivaouane, and it became an important place in my educational journey.

It was in Tivaouane that I began a deeper study of Islamic knowledge. I studied subjects such as Fiqh (Islamic jurisprudence), Nahw (Arabic grammar), Sarf (Arabic morphology), and other Islamic sciences. Throughout this period, I had the opportunity to learn from several teachers and scholars.

Among the teachers who played an important role in my studies were Baye Sal and Saliu Sek. I also developed a particular interest in Arabic grammar. My studies in grammar were guided by Ustaaz Dam and Ustaaz Seriñ Baye, from whom I learned many important principles of the Arabic language.

I especially enjoyed studying Arabic grammar. I continued studying these subjects for many years, gradually building my knowledge and understanding of Islamic sciences and the Arabic language. This period of my life was a major stage in my development as a student of knowledge.

After spending many years pursuing traditional Islamic education, I eventually joined a formal school in Tivaouane. This marked another important stage in my educational journey, as I began combining my traditional Islamic studies with formal education.

My Journey into Formal Education

Before entering formal education, I had spent many years studying in the traditional Dara system. Dara education was an informal form of learning, but it gave me a strong foundation in Qur’anic and Islamic studies. During this period, I studied many important books and subjects, including the Qur’an, Hadith, Nahw (Arabic grammar), and other areas of Islamic knowledge.
